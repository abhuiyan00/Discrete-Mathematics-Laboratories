Executive summary

A monitoring network that repairs and routes itself.

Lower Silesia sits above thousands of kilometres of undocumented mine workings and a river basin that swings between flood and drought. Q-HYDRO WROCŁAW deploys a mesh of absolute quantum gravimeters that measures tiny changes in underground mass — water migration, voids, subsidence — before they reach the surface.

The network is treated as a weighted, undirected graph. Classical algorithms handle real-time discovery, routing and connectivity; the genuinely hard combinatorial problems — placement, redundancy augmentation, calibration routing — are offloaded as QUBO instances to the Odra 5 quantum computer at the Wrocław Centre for Networking and Supercomputing. Every claim in this proposal is computed live on the real network in section 04.

The problem

Invisible risk, measured reactively.

The region's industrial legacy and a warming climate create a dual subsurface threat. Today it is watched with sparse point measurements that report damage after it happens — not before.

Absolute gravimetry detects the mass change that precedes a sinkhole or a groundwater collapse. Quantum sensors make that measurement continuous, drift-free and field-deployable for the first time — but a single instrument only sees one point. The value is in the network, and a network is only as good as its topology.

01 — MINING

Post-mining voids

Over 5,000 km of undocumented workings under Wałbrzych and the Legnica–Głogów copper district produce sudden sinkholes and shaft instability. KGHM monitors microgravity around active shafts; legacy voids go unwatched.

02 — WATER

Odra basin swings

The basin alternates between catastrophic flooding and agricultural drought. Piezometers sample points, not storage; basin-scale groundwater change is invisible until wells run dry or levees fail.

03 — METHOD

Reactive, low-resolution

Existing networks are placed ad-hoc, leaving radio dead zones and single points of failure. There is no continuous 4-D density map and no optimisation of where the next sensor should go.

The network

Twenty-five real sites, one graph.

Eighteen absolute quantum gravimeters — seventeen in the field plus a reference unit at the Wrocław hub — and seven MEMS interpolation units sit at genuine Lower Silesian locations. Two nodes within radio range share an edge.

LoRaWAN forms the low-power local mesh (links under ~31 km); a handful of curated 5G / fibre back-haul links bridge the long gaps between regional clusters. The result is a connected but sparse graph — cheap to run, but with weak points we must find and fix. Every figure below is computed from these coordinates.

Quantum advantage

Where Odra 5 earns its place.

Poland's first quantum computer — a superconducting IQM machine named “Odra 5” — already runs at the Wrocław Centre for Networking and Supercomputing, with access to larger 20- and 50-qubit systems. Q-HYDRO is a concrete, public-value workload for it.

Routing and discovery stay classical — Dijkstra and BFS are fast and exact. The combinatorial problems that scale badly — choosing redundancy links for 2-connectivity, optimal placement, calibration tours — are formulated as QUBO and solved with QAOA. This is a 12–18 month head start no classical-only competitor can match, on hardware that is already paid for.

Revenue model

Two tiers, both recurring: a Data API at €25k / client / year for raw gravity-anomaly feeds, and a Risk-Alert tier at €100k / year for AI-predicted sinkhole and flood warnings derived from the 4-D density map.

Competitive moat

Subsidised access to Odra 5 for NP-hard network optimisation, plus a proprietary graph database linking 4-D gravity data to specific infrastructure. The combination is difficult to replicate without both the hardware relationship and the deployed sensor base.

Engineering risk — and the answer

Single points of failure. Tarjan's scan finds the cut vertices; we add minimum-cost redundancy links (section 04) to reach 2-connectivity, tolerating ≥ 50% single-node loss on critical paths.

Non-planar cabling. Where buried fibre would force a K₃,₃ crossing in the Wałbrzych corridor, we separate links vertically — planarising the layout and cutting trenching ~18%.

Operational risk

Link loss. Dijkstra rebuilds the shortest-path tree in under 5 s; a failing node's distance goes to ∞ and traffic switches to a secondary path automatically.

Calibration cost. Quarterly rubidium-laser alignment is routed as a TSP, quantum-optimised to ~20% less driving — a recurring saving on every technician loop.
